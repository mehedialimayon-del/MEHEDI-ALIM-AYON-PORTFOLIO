My Journey to Malaysia: From Self-Dependence to a New Beginning

After completing my studies at Bangladesh Agricultural University, like many young people, I also dreamed of building my future overseas. But from a very young age, I always had a strong desire to become self-dependent. Depending on my parents financially was never something I preferred.

By the grace of Almighty Allah, I was blessed with parents who could have supported all of my expenses. My father served in the Army, and watching him live a disciplined and self-dependent life taught me one of the most valuable lessons of my childhood: learn to stand on your own feet.

That mindset strongly influenced my decision to go abroad. Europe was one of the options, but the financial requirements were much higher. I believed that Malaysia would allow my wife and me to build our future together with comparatively manageable expenses.

My cousin Meghla Mitu played a major role in helping us prepare everything. With her support, we were able to complete all the arrangements and come to Malaysia within only 22 days.

When we arrived, we heard many negative opinions about Malaysia. Some people told us that the country was very similar to Bangladesh and that finding a good job would be extremely difficult. Naturally, there was fear and uncertainty. But I also had a strong belief inside me that I would find a way.

My wife was a very good researcher and had an excellent academic background. I wanted both of us to have an opportunity to continue our research and eventually pursue PhD studies abroad. So instead of wasting time waiting for opportunities in Bangladesh, we decided to take the risk and start our journey in Malaysia.

After arriving, I was amazed by Malaysia's development. The country's infrastructure, organization and economic progress made me realize how rapidly a country can transform itself through proper planning and development.

Starting From Zero: My First Job in Malaysia

The beauty of Malaysia came with another reality: living expenses were high. Our university was around 22 kilometers from our home, and we had tuition fees, transportation costs, rent and daily expenses to manage. Since we had brought limited funds from Bangladesh, I knew immediately that I had to start earning.

For the first few days, the financial pressure was extremely high. We barely went outside because every expense felt significant. I knew that I could not simply sit and wait for something better to happen.

Eventually, I found a job at a restaurant. I was told that I would work as a cashier for RM8 per hour and that the first ten days would be a training period.

But the reality was very different. I had to open and clean the restaurant, serve customers, deliver food, close the restaurant, prepare coffee and drinks, and learn different food preparation tasks. I often worked continuously for 10 to 12 hours while standing.

I still remember asking myself: “Where have I come from, and what am I doing now?” I knew my education and capabilities, but I was in a foreign country where I did not even know where to look for a professional opportunity.

There were not many Muslim food options around my workplace. There was a 7-Eleven nearby where I could buy a bun. Many days, that bun became my lunch.

But this difficult period taught me something that I still carry today: no honest work is small, and difficult beginnings can create the strongest foundations for future success.

I believe the people who experience real struggle after coming overseas often develop the strongest determination to succeed. That experience became one of the most important lessons of my life.

The PRAN Opportunity: From a Restaurant to Sales Management

One day, while I was living in Malaysia, I met a PRAN salesperson working near my residence. During our conversation, I heard about PRAN's business operations in Malaysia for the first time.

I was surprised because I did not even know that such a large Bangladeshi company had such an extensive presence in Malaysia. I immediately asked him to inform me if there was any job opportunity.

He told me that there was a vacant sales route and asked whether I was genuinely interested in working. I said yes without hesitation. He then called his manager and asked me to come to the office.

That opportunity became a turning point in my life. I travelled by MRT to the office and met the team. I was introduced to Mr. Parvez Hira Sir, Head of Sales – Modern Trade.

At that time, Sir was about to leave for the market. I greeted him and spoke with him for only two or three minutes. By the grace of Almighty Allah, something about my personality and confidence made a positive impression on him.

He called a manager and recommended me, saying that he believed I could perform well. I was given the Bukit Bintang route and asked to start training.

At that time, I did not even properly know the Bukit Bintang area. But I accepted the challenge and started learning the market from the ground up.

My initial route target was around RM100,000, while the existing sales were approximately RM45,000. In my very first month, by the grace of Allah, I achieved around RM83,000 in sales.

My performance received significant recognition. At our National Sales Meeting, I was announced as a Best Performer. Sir also encouraged me and told me that if I continued performing well, I would have the opportunity to become a manager.

I continued working with dedication and became a Best Performer for seven consecutive months in Modern Trade.

From Salesman to Key Account Manager: The Journey Continues

Eventually, a managerial position became vacant, and Mr. Parvez Hira Sir told me to start preparing because I would be taking responsibility from the following month.

Around the same time, I had the opportunity to meet our Managing Director, Mr. Mohammad Selim Bhuiyan Sir. He asked me whether I could handle the responsibility and whether I could drive. I confidently told him that I could, and that I had my own driving experience.

Sir then spoke with the Head of Sales regarding my promotion. Later, I also had the opportunity to meet our CEO, Mr. Ashraf Khan Chowdhury Sir of PRAN-RFL Group. After speaking with me, he appreciated my education, personality and potential and asked the management to consider where I could be best utilized.

The management already had a plan for my growth. Within approximately seven months, I was promoted to Manager.

Later, I became a Regional Manager for the Kuala Lumpur Zone. As a Regional Manager, I achieved back-to-back strong performance for four consecutive months and broke previous performance records in the Kuala Lumpur Zone, delivering massive sales growth above the target.

With the support and trust of my Head of Sales, Managing Director, CEO and my entire team, I continued to grow. In approximately one and a half years, I received multiple promotions and eventually took responsibility as a Key Account Manager.

This journey is one of the proudest achievements of my professional life. It is also a source of pride for my family that I was able to progress from a newcomer in Malaysia to a Key Account Manager within such a short period.

My journey is still continuing. I know there is much more to learn, achieve and contribute. The experiences I have gained—from Bangladesh Agricultural University, Eco Expo, the restaurant, PRAN and Pinnacle Foods—have shaped the person and professional I am today.

I am grateful to everyone who trusted me, supported me, guided me and gave me an opportunity when I needed it most.

InshaAllah, I will continue working with my highest dedication, integrity and capability to contribute to the growth of the company and to achieve bigger goals together.

Please keep me in your prayers.
